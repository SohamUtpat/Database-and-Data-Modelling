1. Real-World System Where Soft Deletes Are Required
Example: E-Commerce Platform (Amazon / Flipkart–like system)

Description:
In an e-commerce system, users can delete products, orders, reviews, or even their accounts. However, deleting this data permanently is often not desirable immediately.

Why Soft Deletes Are Required:

    Audit & Compliance:
    Orders and transactions must be preserved for legal, taxation, and auditing purposes.

    Data Recovery:
    A seller may accidentally delete a product or listing and later want it restored.

    User Experience:
    Deleted user accounts may be reactivated within a grace period.

    Historical Records:
    Sales reports, analytics, and customer behavior analysis depend on past data.

    Referential Integrity:
    Orders may reference users or products; soft delete avoids breaking relationships.

How It Works Conceptually:

    The record is marked as inactive or deleted instead of being removed.

    The data is hidden from normal users but still exists in the database.

    Admins or systems can restore the data if needed.

Conclusion:
Soft deletes are essential when data recovery, auditing, and historical tracking are important.

2. Real-World System Where Hard Deletes Are Preferred
Example: Temporary Cache / Session Management System

Description:
Systems like login sessions, OTP records, temporary tokens, or cache storage handle short-lived and non-critical data.

Why Hard Deletes Are Preferred:

   No Long-Term Value:
   Once a session expires or an OTP is used, the data is useless.

   Performance Optimization:
   Keeping unnecessary records increases storage and slows queries.

   Security Reasons:
   Sensitive data like tokens should be removed completely to avoid misuse.

   High Data Volume:
   Temporary systems generate massive data continuously; soft deletes would waste space.

   No Recovery Requirement:
   Deleted data is never needed again.

How It Works Conceptually:

   Data is permanently removed once it expires or is invalid.

   No restoration or historical tracking is required.

Conclusion:
   Hard deletes are best when data is temporary, sensitive, and irrelevant after use.
